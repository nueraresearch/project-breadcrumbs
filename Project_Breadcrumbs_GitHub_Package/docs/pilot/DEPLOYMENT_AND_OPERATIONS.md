# Deployment and Operating Procedure — Draft

**Status:** Proposed operating concept; requires review and field validation

## 1. Deployment model

Project Breadcrumbs is intended as a voluntary program. A participating homeowner authorizes use of a research package and places it on their own porch or another location they control. The package is used as a controlled research deployment to observe package-related events and recovery outcomes.

No device may be placed on another person's property without that person's authorization. Participants must not be encouraged to confront, follow, detain, or investigate anyone.

## 2. Pre-deployment checklist

- Confirm participant has authority over the deployment location.
- Complete written participant agreement and required notices/consents.
- Confirm the specific deployment complies with legal and project review.
- Inspect enclosure, camera aperture, light sensor, battery, and status indicators.
- Confirm unique module and deployment IDs.
- Confirm storage capacity and clock synchronization.
- Confirm camera settings and approved field of view.
- Confirm data-transfer and access controls.
- Record baseline device health.
- Provide participant with contact and stop/suspend instructions.

## 3. Deployment cycle definition

A deployment cycle begins when an inspected module is placed at an authorized site and a deployment record is created. It ends when the module is retrieved/recovered, its records are reconciled, and the module is either cleared for redeployment or retired.

A cycle is planned to run 30 days (D-34). At each boundary the module is retrieved, the cell is swapped, all stored records are offloaded, and the enclosure is inspected before the next cycle begins. A 90-day pilot is three such cycles per module, so 10 modules produce 30 cycles in total. The module is then redeployed to a different host household for the next cycle; a household hosts one deployment only (D-53).

Volunteers collect the sealed box from the project's operating base and return it there at the end of the cycle (D-55). Carriage between the base and the porch is not part of the monitored period: the device is armed at placement, not at collection (HW-32). The base address is never published and does not appear in any repository document.

Recommended states:
- prepared;
- deployed;
- event-recorded;
- retrieved;
- recovered;
- inspected;
- redeployed;
- retired;
- lost;
- failed.

A module can have multiple event records during one deployment. A deployment cycle should not be counted as multiple cycles merely because multiple sensor events occur.

## 4. During deployment

- Do not physically interact with a package after an alert in a way that creates risk.
- Do not pursue or confront a person.
- Do not access or share images except through the approved project process.
- Report malfunction, damage, unexpected capture, or privacy concerns to the project contact.
- Follow the approved check-in schedule.
- Do not attempt to identify anyone from published or collected material, and do not relay such attempts to the project.
- Suspend the deployment if requested by the participant or project lead.

## 5. Recovery and return

The package carries a reward insert to encourage return of the reusable module. The payment is a flat $20 to whoever returns it, no questions asked and no identity required (D-74, amending D-38). The flat amount replaces the earlier documented-replacement-cost basis: nothing has to be priced, and nothing has to be asked of the returner. The wording, funding, redemption method, and legal implications remain undecided. The amount must not appear in public material (THREAT_MODEL §8).

The reward should be structured to recover hardware, not to solicit personal information or encourage confrontation. The project should not promise anonymity unless the redemption process and applicable law support that promise.

Upon return:
- log recovery time and method;
- inspect device condition;
- reconcile event and image records;
- securely transfer approved data;
- clear or retain data according to policy;
- reset and test the module before redeployment.

## 6. Lost or unrecovered device

Define a fixed recovery window before the pilot. At its end, classify the module as unrecovered/lost for analysis, while allowing a later return to update the record.

The movement-event sequence and the last location fix are the record of where a module went after it left the porch. They establish where it was while it still had power, which is not the same as who moved it or where it ended up. Do not send volunteers to search for or retrieve a device from unsafe or unauthorized locations.

## 7. Participant withdrawal

Participants must be able to request suspension of future deployments. The protocol must explain what happens to data already collected, subject to applicable legal obligations and the approved retention policy.
