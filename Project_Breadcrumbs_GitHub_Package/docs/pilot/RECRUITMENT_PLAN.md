## 1. The task

Thirty host households, one deployment each, for thirty days, across ten modules and a ninety-day window. Ten modules deploy on day one; the other twenty placements depend on modules coming back. A household hosts one deployment and is not reused (D-53). Hosts collect the box from the project's collection point and return it there (D-55); the base address is never published.

Recruitment is rolling, not a one-time batch. The first ten hosts must be ready before day one. The rest are recruited as modules return. A module that is never returned leaves the pool, so thirty is a planning target, not a quota (D-70, D-73).

The sample is self-selected. The protocol does not select households on the basis of prior victimisation, and this plan does not either (protocol §4.2). The project expects the sample may over-represent prior victims and reports that as a limitation; it does not use it as a recruitment strategy.

## 2. Rules that do not bend

- **The box observes; it does not protect.** Every recruitment conversation says this, not only the consent form.
- **Taking part is not a way to make another loss less likely.** The project does not claim that hosting a box reduces the chance of a repeat.
- **The recovery payment is for the hardware, not for risk.** It is not payment for taking part, not compensation for a lost package, and not compensation for the risk of a repeat (D-74).
- **No pressure.** A person can decline or withdraw at any time, without a reason. A first conversation is not a signing appointment.
- **No victim targeting.** Do not ask whether someone has had a package taken, and do not recruit from victim lists, crime reports, or social-media threads about a specific theft. If someone volunteers that they were a victim, do not record the details.
- **No protection promise in the materials.** No "protect," "secure," "deter," "stop theft," "catch," "guard," "watch," or "peace of mind" in the name, flyer, web page, social post, or script.
- **Do not publish host identities or addresses.** The study-name mapping is private (D-78).
- **Do not put the recovery payment amount in public recruitment materials.** The amount is disclosed in the participant briefing (D-74).

## 3. Who is eligible

- 18 or older.
- Has authority over the deployment location: owner, or renter with the owner's permission.
- Has a porch or other controlled location where a package can sit for thirty days without blocking access or creating a hazard.
- Willing to collect the box at the collection point and return it at the end of the cycle (D-55).
- Willing to keep the box sealed and not open, adjust, or aim it, whether or not the name check passes (D-77).
- Willing to complete the briefing and consent, including the study-name check (D-78).
- Able to communicate with the project during the deployment, by phone or email.
- One host per household; no second deployment at the same address (D-53).

A person who wants to host because they believe it will protect the porch is not eligible on that basis. Explain the no-protection rule; if they still want to help the study for another reason, they may continue. If protection is the only reason, do not place a box.

Project staff and image reviewers are not recruited as hosts where avoidable. If a reviewer knows a host, the reviewer recuses from that record (D-79); recruitment and image review are separated where possible.

## 4. Where we recruit

After the town meeting (B-03), use general community channels:

- the town meeting itself, with the sign-up sheet and the one-page handout;
- the town hall or community board, with permission;
- the public library, senior center, and neighbourhood association notices, with permission;
- the local newspaper, radio, and community calendar;
- the project website and general social-media posts;
- word of mouth through people who have heard the briefing.

Do not use:

- door-to-door canvassing, because it is hard to deliver the no-protection message without pressure;
- police, sheriff, or carrier endorsement, because it implies a security service;
- victim lists, crime reports, or threads about a specific theft, because that targets prior victims;
- paid ads targeted at people who have searched for package theft, for the same reason.

Public materials carry the one-sentence pitch, the no-protection line, the time commitment, the eligibility points, and a contact route. They do not carry device photographs, study names, host addresses, or the payment amount.

## 5. The message: say this, not that

| Say this | Do not say this |
|---|---|
| "Help us learn what happens to packages left on porches." | "Protect your packages." |
| "The box observes; it does not protect." | "Stop porch theft." |
| "Taking part is not a way to make another loss less likely." | "Peace of mind." |
| "The box may be opened or taken." | "Catch a thief." |
| "The recovery payment is for the hardware, not for risk." | "Get paid for hosting." |
| "Voluntary; you can withdraw at any time." | "Guaranteed." |
| "We cannot promise anonymity from neighbours." | "Anonymous." |
| "The briefing explains what is published." | "Your face will never be published." |

Do not use holiday theft headlines or "tis the season" framing. It implies the box is a response to theft, which is the opposite of the study's position.

## 6. Answering the hard questions

**"Will this protect my porch?"**
"No. The box is a research instrument. It observes an opening; it does not prevent one, and we cannot promise it reduces the chance of another loss. The recovery payment is for the hardware, not compensation for that risk. If protection is your reason for joining, this is not the right study for you."

**"Why should I do it?"**
"To help the town learn what happens to packages left on porches. It is not a security service, and it will not protect your porch."

**"What do I have to do?"**
"Keep one sealed box on your porch for thirty days. Do not open it, adjust it, or aim it. Collect it from the project's collection point and return it at the end. Report it if it is opened or damaged. Do not confront or follow anyone."

**"What about the payment?"**
"There is a recovery payment for returning the hardware, with no questions asked. It is not payment for taking part, and it is not compensation for a lost package or for the risk of a repeat. The amount is in the briefing, not in public materials."

**"Will you publish my face?"**
"The project publishes images under its rules. Images of minors and people unconnected to the opening are withheld. The adult who opens the box may be published. We cannot promise anonymity from neighbours. The briefing explains this before you sign."

**"I have had a package stolen. Is this for me?"**
"I'm sorry that happened. Taking part will not make another loss less likely. If you want to help the study, we would be glad to explain it, but please do not join for protection. If you are looking for a security measure, this is not one."

**"Can I think about it?"**
"Yes. Nothing is signed today. Read the briefing, talk to your household, and come back with questions."

## 7. The pipeline

- **First ten hosts** before day one, one for each module. The launch is gated on ten consented hosts; if ten are not ready, delay the launch rather than starting with a partial fleet. A partial start is a protocol deviation, recorded with its reason.
- **Twenty rolling placements** as modules return, each at a new household.
- **Buffer:** plan for at least two enquiries for every placement and a standby list of at least ten eligible hosts. These are planning targets, not measured conversion rates; adjust them from the observed pipeline.
- **Weekly review:** enquiries, briefings, consented hosts, placements, standby, withdrawals. Do not record reasons for declining.
- **If more than thirty:** waitlist them. Do not add placements beyond the budget or reuse a household.
- **If a host withdraws before placement:** replace from standby. If after placement, follow the withdrawal procedure; the module is collected and not returned to that porch.

## 8. Roles and separation

| Role | Responsibility |
|---|---|
| Recruitment lead | Runs channels and enquiries, schedules briefings, keeps the pipeline. Not an image reviewer where possible. |
| Briefing and consent | Reads the participant packet, answers questions, completes the consent and study-name check. May be the recruitment lead, but not an image reviewer for that host. |
| Logistics | Schedules collection and return, inspects the box, records the deployment. |
| Image reviewer | Separate from recruitment. If they know a host, they recuse from that record (D-79). Host identities are not shared with reviewers. |
| Study Lead | Approves materials, reviews the first five briefings, tracks the pipeline, and ensures the no-protection message is delivered. |

## 9. What we record

The recruitment tracker is part of the participant contact record, not a separate store. It records:

- date and channel of the enquiry;
- a contact handle, not a copy of the person's life story;
- eligibility yes/no;
- briefing date and consent version;
- study-name check and date (D-78);
- placement date and status;
- withdrawal date, if any.

It does not record prior victimisation, theft history, or a reason for wanting to join. If someone volunteers that they were a victim, the details are not written down. The retention of contact records follows DPC §5.

## 10. Checks against the protection implication

- Every flyer, web page, email, and social post is reviewed against the table in §5 before use.
- Every briefing includes the §2.7 callout and the §2.9 payment sentence from the participant packet.
- The Study Lead reviews the first five briefings and any new wording.
- If a host says they expect protection, pause and re-read the no-protection section. If they still expect it, do not place a box. Record that the briefing was repeated, not the person's reason.
- Track misunderstandings and complaints. If a pattern appears, revise the materials before continuing.
- Do not show the device or describe its construction in recruitment. A device demonstration reads as a security product.
- Do not use the word "protect" except to say it does not.

## 11. If recruitment falls short

- If the launch gate is not met, delay. If the ninety-day window forces a partial start, record it as a deviation and report the reduced exposure.
- Add channels and extend the recruitment window within the ninety days.
- Use the standby list.
- Reduce the number of placements and report the achieved exposure as a feasibility result. Do not present it as the planned sample.
- Do not relax eligibility, pressure a hesitant host, imply protection, or raise the recovery payment to fill a quota. A change to the payment is a protocol and ethics change, not a recruitment tactic.
- If a module is never returned, it leaves the pool. The remaining placements are fewer; the report says so (D-70).

## 12. Timeline

- **Before launch:** approvals (B-03 to B-06), participant packet approved, materials reviewed, information page live, first ten hosts briefed and consented.
- **Launch week:** town meeting, community notices, first briefings, first ten placements.
- **Rolling weeks:** briefings, placements, standby management, weekly pipeline review.
- **Close:** thirty placements or the ninety-day/budget stop, whichever comes first; report the achieved number and the limitations.
