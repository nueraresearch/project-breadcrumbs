## 1. How to use this document

- **Part A** is the briefing. Read it with the participant, or give it to them in advance. Answer questions. Do not proceed if they do not understand it.
- **Part B** is the consent form. The participant initials each statement and signs. Give them a copy. Record the consent version in the participant record.
- **Part C** is the withdrawal procedure. Explain it before signature; it is also summarised in Part A.
- **Part D** is the staff checklist. Complete it before the box is placed.
- **Part E** lists what must be closed before this form is used. It is not ready now.
- One household hosts one deployment (D-53). Do not reuse this form for a second deployment at the same address.

## 2. Part A — What you need to know

### 2.1 What this study is

Project Breadcrumbs is a small research pilot in Monte Vista. About 30 households will each keep one sealed box on their porch for 30 days. The project wants to learn what happens to packages left on porches: whether they are opened, moved, and where they go.

This is a feasibility study. It cannot estimate a theft rate for the town, and it will not identify anyone. It is not a police operation or a security service.

### 2.2 What you are asked to do

Keep one sealed box on your porch for 30 days. Do not open it. At the end, return it to the project's collection point in town. The box is armed when it is placed on your porch, not while you carry it.

### 2.3 The one rule: do not open the box

> **Do not open the box.** The name on the label is a study name, not your name. Do not cut the tape, lift the lid, or look inside. Do not adjust or aim it. Ask everyone in your household not to open it. Keep it sealed until the project collects it.

If you find the box open or damaged, do not reseal, repair, or investigate it. Report it and the project will collect it. If it must be moved for safety, move it as little as possible and tell the project. If you or someone in your household opens it by mistake, tell the project; it is recorded as a protocol deviation, and the image is handled under the project's image rules. If mail arrives for the study name, do not open it; return it to the carrier or mark it return to sender.

### 2.4 What the box records

The box contains a small camera and sensors. The camera is blind while the lid is closed. When the box is opened, a light trigger takes up to three still images. There is no audio, no video, and no continuous recording, and the project does not use facial recognition.

The camera looks out at whoever opens the box. A photograph may show the person and what is behind them, including part of your porch. The box also records event times, device health, and location fixes. Images are encrypted at rest, and before release they are seen only by named reviewers; access is logged. A reviewer who knows you or your household does not review your images. If another person appears in a frame, a second reviewer must agree before any image is published.

### 2.5 What is published

The project publishes its event records and images. Published records do not include your name, your address, or a precise coordinate. Location is published as a grid cell of 1 km or coarser. Publication is delayed; the current delay is 30 days.

Captions say a package was opened. They never say a theft occurred, and they carry no names or identification requests. Images of a minor, or a person with no connection to the opening, are never published; the number withheld is published by category. Only frames covered by an image review are released, so a frame that arrives later is not published unreviewed.

A photograph may show part of your porch or background. The project reviews images before release and withholds images of minors and people unconnected to the opening, but it cannot promise that no identifying detail will appear in a photograph. If there is something on your porch you do not want photographed — a house number, a name on a sign, a document — tell the project before placement. The project will consider it when placing the box.

Published data is public. Anyone can read it, including police, and the project cannot control what they do with it. Publication is permanent: once something is released, it cannot be recalled.

### 2.6 Law enforcement

The project does not proactively give your data to police. If police or a court require it, the project will comply with valid legal process after review by counsel, and will tell you unless the law prevents it. Published data is already public.

### 2.7 What participation does not do

The box observes an opening; it does not prevent one. It is not a security system, a police service, or a guarantee that a package will not be taken. The project does not ask you to confront, follow, detain, or identify anyone. If you believe there is immediate danger, call 911 or your local emergency number. Do not retrieve the box from an unsafe place.

### 2.8 Risks

- The box may be opened or taken. Leaving a package on a porch can attract the same attention as any package.
- You may feel anxious or exposed, or neighbours may ask questions.
- A photograph may show you, the person who opened the box, or part of your porch, and may be published.
- A neighbour who moves the box, or a delivery person, may be recorded. The project does not treat a movement as proof of theft, and it does not identify anyone.
- The box may be damaged or may not work.
- The study may not produce useful results.

### 2.9 Payment

The project pays a flat $20 to whoever returns the module, with no questions asked and no identity required. This is a recovery payment for the hardware. It is not payment for taking part, not compensation for a lost package, and not payment for information. If the module is never returned, no payment is made. Please do not publicise the amount; a public offer could encourage someone to take a box in order to return it.

### 2.10 Your choices and withdrawal

Taking part is voluntary. You can say no now, or withdraw at any time, without giving a reason. Withdrawal stops any future deployment for you; the project will not return the module to your porch. If the box is still with you, the project will collect it at a time you agree to, and no later than the next scheduled collection visit.

You can ask for your event and image records to be deleted. Deletion is honoured unless a legal hold applies; if a hold applies, the project tells you in writing why and for how long. Anything already published cannot be recalled. If the module is returned, the $20 recovery payment is still paid.

**What happens to data already collected:** your event and image records are kept under the project's retention schedule; you may ask for them to be deleted, deletion is honoured unless a legal hold applies, and anything already published cannot be recalled.

### 2.11 Contact

Project lead: [name] · [phone] · [email]. Collection point: given at the briefing; the address is not published. If you have a concern during the deployment, contact the project. If you believe there is immediate danger, call 911.

## 4. Part C — Withdrawal: what happens

A spoken request is enough. The participant does not need a reason, and the project does not record one.

| Step | What happens |
|---|---|
| How to withdraw | Tell the project in person, by phone, by email, or through the collection point. A spoken request is enough. |
| Record | The project records the date and channel. It does not record a reason. |
| Immediate effect | No future deployment is planned for the participant, and the module is not returned to that porch. |
| If the box is still with the participant | The project collects it at a time agreed with the participant, and no later than the next scheduled collection visit. It is not redeployed. |
| Data request | The participant may ask for their event and image records to be deleted. The project records the request. |
| Deletion | Deletion is carried out under the retention schedule. It is honoured unless a legal hold applies. If a hold applies, the project tells the participant in writing why and for how long. |
| Published data | Anything already published stays published. It cannot be recalled, and counts already released cannot be changed. |
| Payment | If the module is returned, the $20 recovery payment is still paid. It is for the return, not for staying in the study. |
| Confirmation | The project tells the participant in writing if a legal hold prevents deletion, and what was retained. |

**The sentence to use, exactly:** "Your event and image records are kept under the project's retention schedule; you may ask for them to be deleted, deletion is honoured unless a legal hold applies, and anything already published cannot be recalled."

## 5. Part D — Staff checklist before signature

- Confirm the participant has authority over the deployment location.
- Confirm the study name has been assigned and checked with the participant, and record the date (D-78).
- Read the briefing; answer questions. Do not proceed if understanding is unclear.
- Confirm the participant has told the people in their household not to open the box, as far as they can.
- Confirm the participant knows the box must stay sealed and must not be moved or aimed.
- Explain withdrawal, including the collection arrangement and the deletion request.
- Complete the consent form; record the version; give the participant a copy.
- If the study name is reissued after the check, use a new form; do not alter a signed one.
- Confirm the collection point and the collection date.
- Confirm the box is sealed, labeled with the study name, and carries no project mark (D-39).
- Do not promise protection, a specific result, or a payment beyond the $20 recovery payment.
- Ask about anything on the porch the participant does not want photographed; adjust placement if possible.
- Store the consent form and contact details separately from device data.
- Do not publish the participant's address or the $20 amount.

## 6. Part E — Before this form is used

These are blocking. The form is a draft until each has an answer.

- **Legal review (B-04, D-54).** Consent, notice, recording, and publication terms reviewed by qualified counsel. This document is not legal advice.
- **Ethics review determination (B-05).** In writing.
- **Insurance and liability (B-06).** A statement to insert into the briefing; the project's arrangements are not described here because they are not settled.
- **Retention period and deletion mechanism (C-06).** Insert the actual period; implement the deletion path. Do not sign a blank.
- **Contact details and collection point.** Fill in the placeholders. Keep the operating base address out of this repository and out of public material.
- **Withdrawal collection procedure.** Name who collects, and the fallback if the participant asks for same-day collection.
- **Law-enforcement position (DPC §7).** The draft states the proposed research-only, compelled-process-only default. Confirm it is adopted before use; if the owner chooses another position, §2.6 and consent statement 12 must change.
- **Translation and accessibility.** Arrange interpretation or another format before proceeding if needed. Do not rely on a family member to interpret.
- **Publication pipeline alignment.** [`DATA_PROTECTION_CONTROLS.md`](../governance/DATA_PROTECTION_CONTROLS.md) §8 requires event times to be published in coarse bins; [`../../server/publish.py`](../../server/publish.py) currently publishes exact `sensor_ts`. Resolve before any release. This is a publication blocker, not a signing blocker, because the consent text does not promise coarse timestamps.
- **Background-identifier check.** The re-identification review recommends a check for house numbers, plates, and interiors (P-04). The consent says the project cannot promise that no identifying detail appears in a photograph; if the check is added, update the sentence.
- **Study-name register and label procedure (D-78).** The form assumes a name is assigned and checked before signature.
- **Town meeting approval (B-03)** is the gate to fielding, not to signing, but no deployment may occur without it.
- **One deployment per household (D-53).** Do not reuse this form for a second deployment at the same address.
